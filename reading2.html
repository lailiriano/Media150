I agree with this article because I also feel strongly about the misuse of AI. I find this kind of technology nearly unavoidable, so considering the direction we are heading in, I believe we should use it as a tool for improvement rather than completely villainizing it. At this point, AI is so integrated into everyday life that trying to reject it entirely feels unrealistic. It shows up in the apps we use, the content we consume, and even the way information is shared. Because of that, I think the more productive approach is learning how to guide it responsibly instead of acting like it can simply be removed. However, I do think AI is being misused, and we need more regulations to prevent the wrong people from having unlimited access to create anything they desire. Without boundaries, it becomes too easy for people to manipulate information, spread false narratives, or create harmful content without accountability. This lack of control is what makes AI feel less like a helpful tool and more like something unpredictable. There needs to be a balance between innovation and responsibility, because right now it feels like the technology is advancing faster than the rules meant to contain it.One point that stood out to me was AI being labeled as the new aesthetic of fascism. That idea stuck with me because it highlights how technology is not neutral—it reflects the values of the people using it. The technology is being used to spread highly fascist and conservative ideals that often portray the white man as a savior and promote harmful gender roles. These patterns are not random; they come from the data AI is trained on and the intentions of those controlling it. When those biases are left unchecked, they become amplified, reaching larger audiences and reinforcing ideas that are already damaging.The line between what is AI and what is reality is blurring very quickly because constant usage feeds the technology more information, allowing it to refine its algorithm and produce increasingly hyperrealistic content. This makes it harder to distinguish what is authentic and what is artificially generated, which can be especially dangerous in areas like media, politics, and social issues. When people can no longer trust what they are seeing, it creates confusion and opens the door for manipulation on a much larger scale. I believe that giving this level of freedom to such an intelligent technology could take a negative turn and impact us in ways we may not be able to recover from. Still, I don’t think the solution is fear or complete rejection. Even setting aside its harmful effects on the planet, I think that with proper precautions and regulations, we can still use this technology in ways that ultimately benefit us. It has the potential to support creativity, improve efficiency, and expand access to knowledge, but only if we are intentional about how we control and limit it.
